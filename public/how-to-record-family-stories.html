How to record family stories

The recording is the easy part. The hard part is doing it in a way your great-grandchildren will still be able to open.

A family story that exists only in someone's head is a story on loan. This guide covers the three parts that matter: capturing the story well, capturing it completely, and storing it where it survives — which is the part almost everyone gets wrong.

1. Capture: the phone is enough

You do not need equipment; you need permission and comfort. The voice memo app on any phone produces recordings your family will treasure — the voice is the point, not the fidelity. Two small upgrades matter more than any microphone: put the phone down (holding it changes how people talk), and tell the person exactly what will happen with the recording. People freeze when a recorder appears and thaw when they know where the story is going.

2. Interview like a listener, not a host

The best family recordings come from following, not leading. Let one story finish before the next question. Ask "what happened next" more than "why." Silence is a tool — count to five before filling it; the second half of an answer is usually the story. And write down the date, the place, and who was in the room at the time: that metadata is half the value later.

3. Save for the hundred-year problem

Recordings die in three ways: the app shuts down, the file format goes stale, or the family forgets where they are. The defenses: keep the original file (not just an app's copy), store copies in more than one place, and — most important — attach the story to a person and a date in one family place, like the milestone vault or a time capsule, so future-you finds it by remembering the person, not by remembering a folder name.

4. Transcribe the best ones

Audio is intimate but unsearchable. A short written version of each story — even a paragraph — makes it findable forever and gives the family something to read at gatherings. Pair the transcript with the recording; each covers the other's weakness. Our guide to questions worth asking is a good place to build your interview list.
