Preserving old family photos

Photographs die three ways: physically, digitally, and — most quietly — when nobody can name the faces anymore. Preserving them means beating all three.

A family photo collection is usually the single most irreplaceable thing in the house, and it is almost always stored in the worst box in the worst room. Preservation has three jobs: stop the physical decay, make digital copies that can't be lost the same way, and — the part everyone skips — write down who is in the picture while someone still knows.

1. Stop the physical decay

  • Move them out of the attic, the basement, and the garage — temperature swings and humidity are the enemies.
  • Replace adhesive albums and rubber bands: both destroy emulsion over decades. Archival boxes and acid-free sleeves are the upgrade; plain pencil (never ink) on the sleeve edge for notes.
  • Handle old prints by the edges, and unframe anything that was framed against plain cardboard — it acid-burns prints from behind.

2. Digitize properly

Scan at 600 dpi minimum for prints you care about — enough resolution to print again from the scan. Scan the backs of photos with handwriting on them; the notes are half the record. Phone photos of photos are fine for sharing, not for preservation. Name files by what they are — 1953-grandma-millie-garden.jpg — not IMG_4471.jpg; filenames are the metadata that survives every app.

3. Beat digital decay

The 3-2-1 rule: three copies, two different places, one somewhere else entirely. A laptop, an external drive, and a cloud folder clears the bar. A collection that exists in one place — any one place — is a collection with an expiration date. Give the family copies too: photos stored by multiple branches of the family are functionally immortal.

4. Label while it's possible

The quietest way photo collections die is when the last person who can name the faces goes. Sit down with the oldest photo-knower in your family, once, with the whole collection, and write names, approximate years, and places. Record that session — it always turns into a story session. Then attach the labeled photos to people and dates in the family vault so the names travel with the pictures forever.
