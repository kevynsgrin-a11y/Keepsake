Family milestones, by age

Baby books cover the first year and abandon you at thirteen. This list runs the whole arc — including the years nobody thinks to write down.

The family milestone industry stops at the nursery door — first steps, first words, first birthday, and then silence until graduation. But the years people later wish they had recorded are everywhere: the opinionated eight-year-old, the new-at-work twenty-five-year-old, the grandparent whose stories are the family's archive. Here is a family-first milestone list for the whole arc. Record these against dates in the heritage calendar and file the artifacts in the vault.

First years

  • Arrival — the day, the weather, who cried
  • First laugh (not first smile — the laugh is rarer)
  • The first food that was refused with conviction
  • First word, and the family word that followed

School years

  • The drawing that summed them up that year
  • What they wanted to be, asked every birthday on video
  • First best friend, and the day they met
  • The teacher who changed something

Adolescence

  • The obsession of the year — sport, band, game, fandom
  • First job and what the first paycheck went to
  • The trip that became a family legend

Adulthood

  • Leaving home — what was packed, what was left
  • Weddings and the toasts that mattered
  • First home, first garden, first spectacular failure of a recipe
  • The arrival of the next generation — and the phone call announcing it

Elder years (the ones nobody records)

  • The stories told more than twice — those are the keepers
  • The recipe only they can make
  • Their answers to the grandparent questions · Questions to ask your grandmother · Questions to ask your grandfather · Family recipe questions · Holiday gathering questions · Questions to ask a dying loved one · Questions to ask your parents · Family reunion questions
  • The letter they'd write to the family in fifty years — ask them to write it
The rule that beats the list: a milestone is anything someone will want the date of later. When in doubt, write it down with the date and a sentence of context. A capsule sealed with one artifact per year becomes the family's own museum.
