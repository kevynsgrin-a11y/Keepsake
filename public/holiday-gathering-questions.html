Holiday gathering questions

The holidays are the few days a year when every generation of a family sits at the same table. They are also the most wasted opportunity in family history — everyone talks about the food. These questions change the subject.

Every guide in this family works the same way: ask slowly, one question at a time, and write down the year you asked. The related guides — questions to ask your grandparents, how to record family stories, and the full guide index — cover the rest of the method.

The openers (before the plates are cleared)

  • What was this table like when you were a kid — who sat where, and who cooked?
  • What did this family eat at the holidays that nobody makes anymore?
  • What is the oldest holiday tradition we still keep, and who started it?
  • What did you get for Christmas the year you remember best?

For the oldest generation, while everyone listens

  • What did your family do for the holidays during the hard years?
  • Who hosted when you were young, and what happened to that house?
  • What has been said at this table that people still bring up?
  • Who is missing from this table that used to be here — tell us about them?

The table rounds

  • What is a holiday story this family always tells, and who remembers it differently?
  • What did the aunts and uncles let you do that your parents never knew?
  • What is the family dish that has been made wrong for twenty years, and what is wrong with it?
  • What should we ask Grandpa/Grandma next year that we keep forgetting to ask?

The ones that build the record

  • Who has the oldest photos of these gatherings, and where are they?
  • What recipes on this table came from someone not here to make it?
  • What new tradition should we start that the youngest will remember?
The one-rule method: one question per course, asked once, then let the table argue — the argument is the record. Assign one person to note who answered and roughly when. Next year, read last year's answers back at the same meal; it is the cheapest time machine a family can build.
