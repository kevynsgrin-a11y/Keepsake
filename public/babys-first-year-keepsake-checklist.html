Baby's first year: the keepsake checklist

The first year produces a metric ton of candidate keepsakes and no manual. This is the manual — short on purpose.

Nobody warns you about the volume. The first year of a child's life generates more potential keepsakes than the next twenty combined, and exhaustion makes "keep everything" feel like the only safe answer. It isn't — but the fix is a checklist, not willpower. Here is the one, in order of the year.

The hospital days

  • The bracelet, the tiny hat, the footprint card if they made one
  • The announcement — card, text screenshot, or the photo you actually sent
  • One detail written down that day: the weather, the nurse's name, what you ate first

Monthly

  • The monthly photo — same spot, same pose if you can manage it; the sameness is what makes the series
  • A note on the month: what they love, what they've learned, what's hard (write the hard part too — future-you will treasure the honesty)
  • Growth marks if you're keeping a chart or a doorframe line

The firsts (be selective, be honest)

  • First laugh — record it if you can; it's the audio equivalent of the blurry laughing photo
  • First food, especially the face made at it
  • First word and the mispronounced versions that follow — write them phonetically
  • A handprint or footprint pair, once, around the birthday

The two over-keeps

1. Clothes. Keep exactly two outfits — the coming-home one and the favorite. The rest is textile. 2. "First everything." After the first spoon, the second spoon is a spoon. One artifact per first is plenty; the memory-box rules apply to babies too.

File it all by date as you go — the vault and calendar exist precisely so the first year doesn't end as a shoebox someone has to decode in 2045. And on the first birthday: write the baby a letter. It's the one keepsake they'll be able to read before they can read anything else you saved.
